# An Unnamed Attendant: Continuing to Read the Sutra

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

During the Tang Wude era, Su Chang was appointed prefect of Bazhou and travelled to his post with his household. The Xianying lu says that their vessel encountered wind in the middle of the Jialing River and that more than sixty men and women were lost. Among those aboard was an attendant who regularly read the sutra. Her name is not recorded.

According to the text, when water entered the vessel, she placed the sutra case on her head and vowed to sink with it. The story then says that she did not sink: she floated with the waves, soon reached the bank, and carried the case ashore. On opening it, she found the scripture dry. This is a religious account of rescue, not an independently established description of navigation or physical effects. Her survival should not obscure the deaths of the others reported in the same passage.

The entry closes by saying that she later married in Yangzhou and became still more devoted to reading and recitation. She is an attendant in the prefect’s household, not his daughter, and is not presented as a monastic. The limited biographical evidence thus concerns an unnamed woman whose practice continued after marriage.

The source does not tell us how she learned to read, who taught her, or what her married household was like. This draft leaves those questions open rather than supplying a fuller life story that the evidence cannot support.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

How much of a person’s name or biography survives does not determine whether their practice deserves attention. A steady, suitable reading practice is one possible reflection today; the vow made during a shipwreck is not presented as an action to imitate.
