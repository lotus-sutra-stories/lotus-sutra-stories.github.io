# Master Huiming: A Vow Set Westward, "All Saw the Buddha Come"

> Recorded in the "Eminent Monks" section of the *Xianying lu* under "Master Ming of Later Zhou", and again in the Northern Zhou section of the *Chiyan ji* under "The monk Huiming of Northern Zhou". The *Xianying lu* cites the *Xu Gaoseng zhuan* (Continued Biographies of Eminent Monks) as its source. The English rendering follows the fuller *Chiyan ji* version; variants are noted in the textual notes.

## The Story

Master Huiming, lay surname Guo, was a native of Jinyang in Taiyuan, of striking and handsome bearing. At fifteen he recited the Lotus Sutra, and in twenty-five days had the whole fascicle completely mastered. Soon after he received tonsure and devoted himself to the Vaipulya and Samantabhadra repentance rites.

Later he travelled to Mount Xiancheng in Heyang, staying at Shanguang Monastery, where he lectured on the Mahāyāna teachings; wherever his meditative wisdom pointed, listeners submitted without exception. Dai Kui of Jibei once sent him a letter; his learned reply is remembered, together with the original, for its reclusive excellence. The Chan master Fayin of the same commandery was a true friend in virtue; the two went together to study meditation under Chan master Neng at Guoyuan Monastery in Changsha, then returned to Mount Xiancheng.

One evening, taking Fayin's hand as they walked in the pine grove, they looked at each other, laughed and talked, and firmly set their resolve — a vow to journey to the West.

In the eleventh month of the third Tianhe year of Northern Zhou (569), Huiming sat cross-legged facing west and passed away reciting the Buddha's name. At the end, everyone present saw the Buddha come to welcome him; he joined his palms and was gone. Uncanny fragrance and music were sensed in more than one place.

Eight days later, the Chan master Fayin also died in seated posture, with auspicious signs in every respect the same as Huiming's.

## Editor's Reflection

The precious phrase in this record is "all saw the Buddha come": it was not he himself who claimed the vision — the whole company present saw it together. The source never names which Buddha came; it says only "the Buddha came". But the cross-legged westward posture, the recitation of the Buddha's name, and the earlier vow "set westward" make the direction and the practice plain. The editor therefore files this account under the Pure Land theme while labelling it *interpreted* rather than *explicit* — the same standard this site applies throughout.

Another moving detail is the two friends setting their vow "laughing and talking" in the pine grove. The great matter of birth-and-death, in the compiler's brush, could be handled with such composure.
