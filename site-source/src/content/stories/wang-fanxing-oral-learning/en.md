# Wang Fanxing: Learning by Ear, Practising in Daily Life

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

Wang Fanxing lived in Linyi and had been blind since childhood. The Records of Evident Responses to the Lotus Sutra says that his mother, out of affection for him, taught him the Lotus Sutra orally. It records a total of seventeen thousand recitations. This is the number reported by the text, not a practice target for readers today.

The account specifically says that he remained blind, yet could go about his daily activities and carry out work such as weaving mats and sewing clothes. Others wondered whether he had attained something unusual, but he would not explain. The passage does not report restored sight, and it does not establish an otherwise unrecorded supernatural ability or teaching method.

He wore simple clothes, ate vegetarian food, and never married. The Xianying lu says that he died at seventy-one and asked for his body to be left in woodland. It then relates an account of his tongue remaining intact and his younger brother Huiyi honouring it. The shorter version in the Chiyan ji also says that he died seated cross-legged. These events after death are presented as reports in Buddhist religious literature.

Alongside that religious testimony, the account preserves a clear example of learning supported by a family member: a mother teaching by ear and a son continuing the practice over time.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

The story invites us to consider access to learning beyond reading print. Listening, reciting together, and suitable assistance may be useful according to individual circumstances. These are present-day editorial reflections, not a prescribed method found in the source.
