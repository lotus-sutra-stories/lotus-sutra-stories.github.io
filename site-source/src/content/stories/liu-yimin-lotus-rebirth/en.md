# Liu Yimin: "By the Power of This Sutra", Passing Away Facing West

> Recorded in the "Laymen" section of the *Xianying lu* under "Liu Yimin of Mount Lu" (with the inline citation "Huiyuan's Lu Shan Collection"), and again in the Jin section of the *Chiyan ji* under "Liu Chengzhi of the Jin". The English rendering follows the fuller *Chiyan ji* version.

## The Story

Liu Chengzhi, styled Zhongsi, was a native of Pengcheng and a descendant of Prince Yuan of Chu of the Han. Thoroughly versed in the classics and the hundred schools, he was especially drawn to Buddhist principles. The court honoured him with the title "Yimin" ("Left-behind Subject") and summoned him to office; he declined each time. Eventually he entered Mount Lu and wholeheartedly took refuge with the master Huiyuan. Huiyuan, together with Lei Cizong, Zhou Xuzhi, Zong Bing, Zhang Quan and others, formed the White Lotus Society to practise the Pure Land path together, carving their vow into stone; Liu Yimin was honoured as the society's foremost guest. He also composed the "Poems on the Samādhi of Buddha-Recollection" to declare his intent of single-minded recollection and seated meditation.

Within half a year of entering the mountain he saw, in concentration, the Buddha's light suffusing the earth until all turned golden. For fifteen years he practised without cease. One day, while reciting the Buddha's name, he again saw Amitābha Buddha, his jade-white curls of light radiating, comforting, receiving and blessing him, touching his head. On yet another day of recollection he saw himself entering the jeweled pool, lotus flowers white and blue upon water limpid and still. A figure with a halo around his head and a swastika upon his breast pointed to the water and said: "This is the water of the eight virtues; drink of it." Chengzhi drank, and it was sweetness beyond compare. When he woke, the extraordinary fragrance still issued from his pores. He sighed: "The conditions of my rebirth in the Pure Land have arrived."

Thereupon he asked the monks to recite the Lotus Sutra several hundred times, and the monks of Mount Lu all gathered. Facing the sacred images, Chengzhi burned incense, bowed twice, and made this vow: "Through the bequeathed teaching of Śākyamuni I came to know the compassionate father Amitābha. Let this incense first make offering to the Tathāgata Śākyamuni; next to Amitābha Buddha; and third to the Wonderful Dharma Lotus Flower Sutra — for it is by upholding this sutra that I shall attain rebirth. May all sentient beings together be born in the Pure Land."

With his vow complete, he faced west, joined his palms, and passed away — the sixth year of Yixi (410). An extraordinary fragrance filled the air and did not subside for seven days. The master Huiyuan composed an inscription commemorating his virtue.

## Editor's Reflection

Among everything this site collects, this is the account where the two threads of devotion — Lotus and Amitābha — meet most plainly: a layman of the Lotus Society who, at the end of his life, made a threefold offering — to Śākyamuni, to Amitābha, and to the Lotus Sutra — and said in his own words: "By the power of this sutra I shall be reborn." The compilers preserved him precisely to witness to the sutra's merit.

Note, once again, that the source never says in the narrator's own voice "he was reborn in Sukhāvatī"; it records his own words of assurance, his vow, and the signs at his passing. This site accordingly labels the rebirth claim *interpreted*, by its standing standard. The two versions differ instructively in detail (see the textual notes) and reward side-by-side reading.
