# Master Tanyi: Samantabhadra Comes to Test Him — a Mind Like the Moon in Water

> Recorded in the "Eminent Monks" section of the *Xianying lu* under "Master Feiyun of Tianyi", and again in the Jin section of the *Chiyan ji* under "The monk Tanyi of the Jin" (which opens with the listening-pheasant episode, S015 in this collection). The English rendering follows the fuller *Xianying lu*.

## The Story

Tanyi, once ordained (his former life is told in the previous account), travelled everywhere to study the Way. He first went to Mount Lu and relied on Master Yuan (Huiyuan), awakening to the purport of the vehicle; then he entered Guanzhong and paid homage to Kumārajīva, taking part in the lecturing and translating of sutras and treatises until he understood without obstruction and won great powers of exposition.

Later he and his fellow-student Tanxue travelled east to Kuaiji and arrived at Mount Qinwang — five peaks and twin ravines, majestic in aspect. There they quarried stone and cut thatch to settle, and Tanyi recited the Lotus Sutra exclusively for nearly a full cycle of twelve years.

One evening, as dusk was falling, a woman in embroidered robes appeared, carrying a bamboo basket with a white piglet and two stalks of garlic, and standing before the master she wept and said: "I am a daughter of a family before the hill; I went into the mountains to gather greens, met a fierce tiger on the road, and fled here. The day is ending, the trees are dark, wolves and jackals roam — I have no way home alive. Might the master permit me one night's lodging?"

Tanyi, mindful of the impropriety, firmly refused. The woman wept and pleaded without cease. Reluctantly he allowed her a bed of grass apart from his own, and covered his head and recited the sutra.

At the third watch she cried out in sudden illness, saying her belly was in violent pain and begging him to look at her. He gave her medicine; the pain grew worse, her screams unbroken: "If the master would but massage my belly, I might find some ease — otherwise I shall die. The Buddha's Dharma takes compassion and skillful means as its root; can the master bear to sit by and refuse to stretch out one hand to save me?"

Tanyi said: "I am a monk under the great precepts — to touch a woman's body, what manner of thing is that?" But the pleading was so urgent that he wrapped his staff's head in cloth and massaged her from a distance. After a little while the woman said: "It is no longer needed."

At dawn the woman stepped into the courtyard — her embroidered robes dissolved into auspicious cloud, the piglet turned into a white elephant, the garlic into twin lotuses. With her feet upon the lotuses, mounted on the elephant, riding the cloud, she said to Tanyi:

"I am the Bodhisattva Samantabhadra. You shall soon return to my assembly, and I have come expressly to test you. I have observed the mind within you: like the moon in water — it cannot be stained."

With these words she faded into the distance. Flowers rained from heaven and the earth trembled; the villagers who saw and heard could not withhold their praise.

That very morning the governor Meng Yi, at his desk since early dawn, saw in the south an auspicious cloud breathing light that shot across his courtyard, with the faint sound of chimes and strings beneath it. On inquiry he received the whole account of Samantabhadra's manifestation to Tanyi, and reported it to the throne together with the master's practice. An imperial decree established a monastery, titled "Fahua" — the Lotus — in the thirteenth year of Yixi of Emperor An of the Eastern Jin (417). That monastery is today's Tianyi Monastery.

Tanyi lived to seventy and passed away on that mountain. The community lacquered his true body and kept it there. Through the persecution of the Huichang era, when Buddhism was abolished, the monks hid his image in the hollow of a tree south of the monastery and it escaped destruction. The Martial and Solemn King of Wuyue conferred on him the posthumous title "Master Feiyun" — Flying Cloud.

## Editor's Reflection

This is the most widely circulated account in this site's "peace in this life" theme, and the most vivid fulfilment of the "Encouragement of Samantabhadra" chapter (S002 in this collection): the sutra promises that one who upholds and recites it "whether walking or standing, I shall mount my six-tusked white elephant… and manifest to make offerings and protect" — what Tanyi received was precisely the white elephant's manifestation, though in the guise of a test.

The design of the test is exquisite: deep night, a lone mountain, a beautiful woman, the plea for lodging, the plea for massage — pressure layered upon pressure; and Tanyi's response has its own measure at every layer — the firm refusal, the separate bed, the covered head and recitation, the medicine, the cloth-wrapped staff. Then the riddle unfolds: the pig becomes the white elephant (the six-tusked elephant in another key), the garlic becomes twin lotuses (purity set against its pungency). "A mind like the moon in water — it cannot be stained" is among the most beautiful words of certification in the whole literature of responses.

Let the reader also notice the historian's discipline of the source: Samantabhadra says plainly "you shall soon return to my assembly" — a prediction that at death he joins Samantabhadra's retinue, not that he is reborn in the Western Pure Land. This site classifies accordingly, refusing to misread this "white elephant response" as a Pure Land rebirth record.
