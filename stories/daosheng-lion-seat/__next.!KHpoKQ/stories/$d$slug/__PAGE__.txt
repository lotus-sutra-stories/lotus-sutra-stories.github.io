> Recorded in the "Eminent Monks" section of the *Xianying lu* under "Master Sheng of Huqiu", and again in the Song section of the *Chiyan ji* under "The monk Daosheng of the Song". The English rendering follows the *Xianying lu*; the *Chiyan ji* variants and Gao Qi's poem are noted in the textual notes.

## The Story

Master Daosheng took his master's surname, Zhu. In the years of young study his discourse was fresh and penetrating, and all who met him yielded to him. He held that the essential thing in entering the Way is wisdom-understanding, and so he bored into the whole canon without sparing himself. Later he went to Guanzhong and received the teaching of Kumārajīva; his questions and arguments surpassed the age, and contemporaries called his understanding "divine".

(The *Chiyan ji* adds: Daosheng first dwelt on Mount Lu in seclusion for seven years, constantly reciting the Lotus Sutra; and later, lecturing at Huqiu — the Tiger Hill — when he reached the point that "even icchantikas possess buddha-nature", the stubborn stones nodded.)

At one period he lived at Bantang, reciting the Lotus Sutra. Suddenly a pheasant began coming regularly to listen. One day it did not come, and Daosheng thought of it with concern. In the night it entered his dream and said: "Through listening to the sutra I have had my recompense changed, and I am now born a son in such-and-such a family. After some years I shall return to serve the master." When he inquired, it was indeed so. Not long after the boy ordained, still in childhood, he died, and was buried in the grove.

One night a light burst from the grave, illuminating the creek and its banks. The villagers marvelled and opened the tomb — and found a tongue, and upon it grew a green lotus. A stupa was raised there, later enlarged into a monastery: today's Bantang Monastery.

Daosheng had advanced the theses: "Merit receives no recompense" and "Awakening is sudden". The textualists of the old learning pronounced them heresy. So Daosheng swore before the assembly: "If what I have established does not contradict the purport of the sutras, then at the end of my life I shall die seated upon the lion seat."

Later the Great Nirvana Sutra was translated, and it confirmed precisely what he had said. Daosheng ascended the seat and expounded; his hearers awoke and rejoiced. As the session was about to close, the chowry suddenly dropped from his hand — and Daosheng passed away sitting upright and composed, as though entered into meditation.

A saying of the time ran: "Sheng and Rui sprang from heaven's innocence" — such were the outstanding ones of the age, and he was of them.

## Editor's Reflection

Daosheng is the classic case in Chinese Buddhist history of "reason against the letter, vindicated at last by the letter": that icchantikas possess buddha-nature, that awakening is sudden — both once reckoned rank heresy; then a new translation of the Nirvana Sutra arrived, and it said exactly what he had said. The "nodding stones" of the *Chiyan ji* and the "lion seat" are Chinese Buddhism's two most famous images of verification, and this site preserves the source-chain of the latter.

The Bantang episode — the tongue bearing a green lotus — is the same type as the pheasant at Fazhi's seat (S015), yet a distinct event: again a bird hears the Lotus and gains human birth, but here one step further — the boy dies young, and from his grave comes the lotus-bearing tongue. This site prints both side by side deliberately, to show the motif's variations within the genre. The closing image — the chowry falling, the master passing seated — contains no word of rebirth; this site accordingly files the account under "peace in this life / insight", with the rebirth field marked "not stated".
