18:T79e,# Lady Lu: Thirty Years of Practice with the Pure Land in Mind

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

The Chiyan ji contains a short entry headed “Lady Lu of the Song.” It identifies her as a woman from Qiantang and the wife of Wang Yu, who held the designation chaoqing. “Lady” renders the honorific yiren in the heading; this draft does not infer a birth date, a precise office for her husband, or the household’s wealth.

The text says that she regularly recited the Lotus Sutra, was deeply committed to the Pure Land, and continued repentance rites and Buddha-recitation for thirty years without interruption. Its wording includes one session of repentance and ten thousand invocations. These are reported features of her practice, not a required daily quota for readers. The short entry does not establish a complete timetable for her life.

During a slight illness, the account says, heavenly drums suddenly sounded. As others wondered at this, she faced west, formed a gesture with both hands, and died. This remains the text’s religious account of her death, not an independently verified event established by the present research.

Her Pure Land intention and westward orientation make the story relevant to that theme. Yet the passage does not directly name Amitabha or have the narrator declare that she had already been born in Sukhāvatī. An interpreted classification therefore preserves the distinction between sustained practice, religious intention, and evidence about a final destination.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

A modest arrangement that can be sustained may be more practical than copying a large number from a historical account. Choosing a manageable period of practice is editorial advice; another person’s thirty years do not become an obligation for the reader.
