18:T86f,# Master Daoang: Declining Tuṣita, Choosing the West

> Recorded in the "Eminent Monks" section of the *Xianying lu* under "Master Ang of Xiangzhou", with the inline citation *Wangsheng lüezhuan* (Abridged Accounts of Rebirth). The *Chiyan ji* does not carry this entry. The English rendering follows the *Xianying lu*.

## The Story

At Kaiyuan Monastery in Xiangzhou there was a master named Daoang, who regularly lectured on the Lotus Sutra.

One day, in the very midst of his lecture, he suddenly saw a host of sages descending from open space, banners and canopies about them and music surrounding them, who announced: "The Tuṣita heaven has come especially to welcome you."

Daoang said: "The bliss of the heavens is still the root of birth-and-death. I have never aspired to be born there. What I aspire to is the West alone." Even as he finished speaking, the heavenly host vanished.

A moment later he heard music resounding from the west, clear and bright, wholly unlike what he had heard before. Daoang said with joy: "The hour has come; I must go. Do you all dwell well, recite Amitābha diligently, and be born together in the pure land."

With these words the incense burner dropped from his hand, and on the high seat he closed his eyes and passed away. The fourfold assembly marvelled deeply.

## Editor's Reflection

This account stands as the mirror of Guanjing's story (S009 in this collection): facing a recorded "welcome from the heavens", Guanjing followed his master's record to Tuṣita, while Daoang declined it in public, stating plainly, "my aspiration is the West." Read together, the two show that in the centuries before the Tang, the question "where does a practitioner go at death?" had more than one live answer.

Daoang's words are direct: "Heaven is the root of birth-and-death." He does not deny the excellence of Tuṣita; he points out that every heaven still lies within the round of birth-and-death. For the modern reader this is the best footnote to the "liberating" character of Pure Land aspiration. The source does not state his rebirth in its own narrative voice, so this site labels the claim *interpreted*.
