18:T7a1,# Shi Heshi: Recitation and Compassion alongside Work

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

Under its section on male lay practitioners, the Xianying lu records Shi Heshi of Shicun in Fenggu Township, southwest of the Tang capital. It describes him as inclined towards goodness from youth and says that he regularly recited the Lotus Sutra passage on peaceful practices, keeping compassion and patience in mind.

The account says that he vowed not to ride horses because the sutra taught compassion for all beings. This was his own way of responding to the teaching, not a rule imposed on every reader. It then identifies him with clerical service as a lingshi. While travelling to and from the capital’s offices, he continued to keep recitation in mind. His practice is thus presented alongside official duties and ordinary movement through the world.

A following phrase, “恐逢相識，行必支經,” is difficult to interpret securely. This draft does not expand it into an invented account of avoiding acquaintances, taking detours, or carrying a book. The entry also relates fragrance at his death and the appearance of a fresh red tongue when a joint burial was opened ten years later. These details remain attributed to the religious source.

Although brief, the story joins recitation to compassion and patience. It does not report Amitabha’s reception or explicitly state that Shi had been reborn in the Western Pure Land; the signs after death alone do not establish such a classification.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

A clear reminder from a text can accompany work and relationships. Attention to patience and consideration for others offers a practical reflection beyond counting recitations. This is editorial reflection, not a claim that the source demonstrates a particular outcome.
