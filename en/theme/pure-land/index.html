Aspiring for Rebirth in the Pure Land

This gallery prioritises accounts in which the sources attest vows for, and claims about, Amitābha Buddha's Western Pure Land of Ultimate Bliss. A deathbed fragrance, light or west-facing death alone does not prove the source asserts rebirth in Sukhāvatī; such accounts are presented separately as related records.

Classification caveat: auspicious signs such as facing west, fragrance or celestial music at death are not automatically equivalent to an explicit statement of rebirth in the Western Pure Land. Inferred cases are always labelled "interpreted".

Master Bacheng: Sixty Years of Recitation, a Single Prayer for the West

Bacheng (printed in the source with a variant glyph of 跋)|Eastern Jin (Chang'an)

Bacheng became a monk at twenty with dull faculties, vowing to recite the Lotus Sutra for rebirth in the West. He memorised a line a day, sometimes half a verse, completing the sutra only at eighty. When a heavenly envoy came to invite him to Trāyastriṃśā he declined — his prayer was solely for Ultimate Bliss. He later dreamt of a jeweled pagoda and the welcoming stairway of the Western jeweled city, arranged a communal offering, asked his disciples whether they saw the thousand Buddhas and smelled the uncanny fragrance, then passed away facing west.

Master Huiming: A Vow Set Westward, "All Saw the Buddha Come"

Huiming|Northern Zhou (d. 569)

Master Huiming of Northern Zhou memorised the Lotus Sutra at fifteen and devoted himself to the repentance rites. Studying meditation with Chan master Fayin, the two walked one evening in a pine grove, talking and smiling as they set their vow westward. In the eleventh month of the third Tianhe year he sat cross-legged facing west reciting the Buddha's name; everyone present saw the Buddha come as he joined his palms and passed away amid uncanny fragrance and music. Eight days later Fayin too died seated, with the same auspicious signs.

Liu Yimin: "By the Power of This Sutra", Passing Away Facing West

Liu Chengzhi, styled Yimin|Eastern Jin (d. 410)

Liu Chengzhi of the Eastern Jin left office for Mount Lu, joining Huiyuan's Lotus Society to practise the Pure Land path together. In the samādhi of Buddha-recollection he repeatedly saw the Buddha's light, Amitābha's welcoming touch, and himself drinking the water of eight virtues in the jeweled pool. At the end he offered incense before the sacred images — first to Śākyamuni, then to Amitābha, and third to the Lotus Sutra: "By the power of this sutra I shall be reborn." He passed away facing west with palms joined; the fragrance lasted seven days.

Master Daoang: Declining Tuṣita, Choosing the West

Daoang|Sui–early Tang (Xiangzhou)

Master Daoang of Kaiyuan Monastery in Xiangzhou regularly lectured on the Lotus Sutra. Mid-lecture one day, a host of sages with banners and music descended from the sky, announcing that the Tuṣita heaven had come especially to welcome him. 'Heaven is the root of birth-and-death,' he replied; 'I have never aspired to it — my aspiration is the West alone.' They vanished. Presently music resounded from the west; he charged the assembly to 'recite Amitābha diligently and be born together in the pure land', the incense burner dropped from his hand, and he closed his eyes on the high seat. The source cites the Wangsheng lüezhuan.

Master Kejiu: Names on the Lotus Platforms, Verified Five Years On

Kejiu|Northern Song (d. after 1093)

Kejiu of Kaiyuan Monastery in Siming constantly recited the Lotus Sutra and was known as 'Master Jiu of the Lotus'. A close friend of Su Shi, he died without illness in 1093 and revived after three days, reporting that he had travelled in spirit to the Pure Land: what he saw matched the Contemplation Sutra exactly, and on the lotus platforms of the jeweled pool were written the names of those yet to be born — a purple-gold platform for the monk Xun (already born), golden platforms for Sun the Twelfth and for Kejiu himself, a silver platform for the nun Xu. Five years on, the nun died with fragrance filling her room; seven years on, Sun died to celestial music — each just as he had said.

Master Guanjing: A Lotus Sutra Career, a Recorded Tuṣita Destination

Guanjing (Zhang'an Zongchi)|Sui–Tang (561–632)

Guanjing of Zhang'an set down in writing every doctrine the Tiantai master Zhiyi expounded. For a dying villager thirty li away he recited the Lotus Sutra and burned sandalwood — the patient smelled it from afar and recovered; he vowed that the islet of Anzhou might be levelled, and white sand welled up smooth as a jade mirror; lecturing the Nirvana Sutra amid sea-raiders, the brigands saw weapon-bearing giants a zhang tall and fled. In 632, ill, with uncanny fragrance in his room, he quoted the Maitreya Sutra ('when the Buddha enters nirvana, incense-smoke like clouds') and passed away. Years earlier the Chan master Zhixi had already recorded a vision: in Tuṣita, beside Zhiyi's seat, one seat stood empty — 'six years hence, Master Guanjing ascends to teach here.' The source cites Gu Shan's Xianju bian.

Project disclosure

Lotus Sutra Stories is an independent, non-commercial, freely accessible Buddhist literature project presenting historical accounts of upholding and reciting the Lotus Sutra, particularly from the Fahua jing xianying lu and Fahua jing chiyan ji. We distinguish primary texts, modern Traditional Chinese explanations, English translations, and editorial reflections. Miraculous events are presented as accounts recorded in religious literature, not as independently established scientific or historical facts or guarantees of particular worldly or rebirth outcomes. This project is not officially affiliated with or endorsed by the religious organizations, archives, databases or institutions referenced. Third-party text transcriptions, translations and artwork remain subject to their applicable licences and attribution requirements.
