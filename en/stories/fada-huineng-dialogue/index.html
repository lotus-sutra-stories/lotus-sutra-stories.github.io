The Monk Fada: 'When the Mind Awakens, It Turns the Lotus Sutra'

Person：Fada; the Sixth Patriarch Huineng

Period：Tang dynasty (Caoxi)

Concerns the Sixth Patriarch Huineng; the Chiyan ji files it under the Tang.

Reading preferences

事見《法華經持驗記》卷一唐朝「唐韶州南華寺釋法達」條。《顯應錄》成書於宋，未收此條。此事亦見《六祖壇經》，文字互有出入（見考證）。以下白話譯註依《持驗記》所錄。

故事

法達，洪州人，七歲出家，出家之後長誦《法華經》。

後來他到曹溪頂禮六祖惠能。祖問：「你誦此經，以什麼為宗？」

法達說：「我只依文念誦，哪裡知道什麼宗趣。」

祖令他取經展開誦讀。法達高聲誦到〈譬喻品〉，祖說：「停。此經以因緣出世為宗。經云：『以一大事因緣出現於世。』一大事者，佛之知見也。若能於相離相、於空離空，一念心開，是為開佛知見。你當信：佛之知見者，即汝自心，更無別物。莫向外求——與佛無二。」

於是說偈：

心迷法華轉，心悟轉法華。 誦經久不明，與義作讎家。 無念念即正，有念念成邪。 有無俱不計，長御白牛車。

法達聽罷，不覺悲泣，也以偈讚答，其中有一句：

經誦三千部，曹溪一句亡。

從此頓悟玄旨。

編者省思

兩部感應錄裡，這是唯一一則「對話式」的記載，也是唯一被禪門收編的故事：法達不是因誦經得瑞相，而是因誦經不得力，被一句話點醒。「心迷法華轉，心悟轉法華」——經還是那部經，人換了一副心腸，主客便整個翻轉。

值得玩味的是「白牛車」的收尾。《法華經》以羊車、鹿車、牛車喻三乘誘引，以大白牛車喻一佛乘；六祖却说「有無俱不計，長御白牛車」——連有無的計較都放下，才配得上坐那輛車。這是《法華》義理在禪門的一次精彩轉讀。誦經三千部而無明未破，與義作「讎家」；本站把這則放在「悟入」類的用意，正在於提醒：感應錄的世界裡，最難得的瑞相，是轉身。

Master Zhicong: Four Tigers Cross with Him; He Passes Away Seated

Zhicong|Sui–Tang transition (533–631?)

When Sui collapsed, Zhicong could find no way home and hid in the reeds by the river, reciting the Lotus Sutra — seven days without hunger, four tigers tame about him. An old man came with a small boat to ferry him across; the tigers crouched and would not leave. 'Are you coming too?' he asked, and they rose, crossed with him to the south bank, where boat and old man vanished. He settled at Qixia Monastery; in the Zhenguan era, ninety-nine years old, he died seated upright, and the tigers went away mourning.

Master Daosheng: Sudden Awakening, Dying Seated upon the Lion Seat

Zhu Daosheng|Eastern Jin (d. 434)

Zhu Daosheng, ordained under Zhu Fatai, dwelt seven years in seclusion on Mount Lu, constantly reciting the Lotus Sutra. He later studied under Kumārajīva in Guanzhong; his dialectic surpassed all, and men called his understanding divine. Lecturing at Huqiu on the theme that even icchantikas possess buddha-nature, the very stones nodded. At Bantang, while he recited the sutra, a pheasant came regularly to listen; he later heard it had been reborn as a boy who ordained, recited the Lotus, and died young — at the grave a light shone one night, and on opening it a tongue was found bearing a green lotus, whereupon a stupa and later a monastery arose (today's Bantang Monastery). His theses — 'merit receives no recompense' and 'awakening is sudden' — the textualists damned as heresy; so he swore before the assembly: if his teaching did not contradict the sutras, he would die seated upon the lion seat. When the Great Nirvana Sutra arrived and confirmed him, he ascended the seat to teach; as the session closed, his chowry dropped from his hand, and he passed away sitting upright, composed as in meditation.
