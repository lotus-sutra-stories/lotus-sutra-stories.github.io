19:T772,# Lady Shen: Listening Steadily within the Household

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

The Xianying lu describes a newly married woman surnamed Shen in the Wu household during the early Shaoxing era. Her mother-in-law recited the Lotus Sutra each morning. Shen attended to her respectfully and came to listen whenever the text was opened—sometimes to one or two chapters, sometimes to a scroll—without growing tired of it.

The account does not say that Shen herself could recite the whole sutra from memory or that she was a nun. The form of practice it preserves is sustained listening within household life. It does not make literacy or a large daily quota a prerequisite.

When she later became ill, she told her mother-in-law that the elder’s recitation had enabled her to hear the teaching. Her worldly ties were ending, she said, and she could no longer continue serving her. She wished that they might meet in an assembly of the Buddhas in another life and left a verse. The source then reports that she died seated cross-legged. Her mother-in-law told Chan Master Hongzhi of Tiantong, who praised the account and provided his own funerary coffin (kan). The entry continues with a religious report of relics after cremation.

Her wish to meet “in an assembly of the Buddhas” does not specify the Western Pure Land. This draft retains the connections between listening, family interaction, and farewell, without adding a determination of rebirth that the passage does not make.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

Listening to a short passage together, with each person free to choose whether to join, is one possible present-day reflection. This particular family account does not establish a requirement that household members practise together.
