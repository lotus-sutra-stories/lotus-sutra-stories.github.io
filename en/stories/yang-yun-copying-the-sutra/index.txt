2a:T79c,# Yang Yun: Copying with Attention and Respect

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

The section on female lay practitioners in the Xianying lu contains an entry headed “Yang Yun, a Courtesan of Huzhou.” It describes her as a changji and says that she copied the Lotus Sutra by hand. Before taking up her brush, she observed a vegetarian discipline, washed, bathed, and changed clothes. The entry gives no age, exact period, or further details about her work.

It then relates that, on the night Yang died of illness, her mother dreamed that she came to say farewell. In the dream, Yang attributed her coming birth as a girl in the household of a Wucheng county clerk named Cai to her sutra-copying. Cai’s wife also reportedly dreamed that night of a sedan chair arriving at the door. She welcomed Yang Yun, who said that she had come to stay. After waking, Cai’s wife gave birth to a daughter.

When Yang’s mother later visited, the account says that the infant smiled at her. The compilation brings together two dreams and this meeting as a religious narrative of rebirth. They are not independently verifiable testimony from living witnesses, and the destination described is a human household, not Sukhāvatī.

One practice remains particularly clear within the short account: Yang copied a scripture and prepared respectfully before writing. This draft does not infer repentance, coercion, or a wish to escape her social position when the passage states none of those motives.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

A person’s recorded actions deserve attention without first assigning value according to social status. Someone wishing to copy a passage today can begin with an accurately sourced text and careful writing. The dream narrative is not presented as a guaranteed reward for copying.
