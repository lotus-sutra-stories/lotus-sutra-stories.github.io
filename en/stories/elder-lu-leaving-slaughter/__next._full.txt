2b:T847,# Elder Lu: Leaving Slaughter and Sustaining a Practice

> This editorial rendering follows the cited source. Extraordinary events are reported as religious narratives, not independently established facts.

## The account

The Chiyan ji describes Elder Lu, a butcher living south of Huzhou during the Song period. At twenty-three, he was visited by a travelling monk who urged him to change his occupation. Lu replied that the trade had been inherited and was difficult to give up immediately. The monk encouraged him to uphold the Lotus Sutra and the Diamond Sutra, then, according to the account, suddenly disappeared.

The text says that Lu reconsidered his life, observed a vegetarian discipline, and vowed to stop slaughtering animals. The account records that he reverently enshrined a painted image of Amitabha, Avalokiteshvara, and Mahasthamaprapta, and sought a teacher to learn the two sutras. In less than five years he could recite them from memory. He then offered incense each day, recited a complete Lotus Sutra and a scroll of the Diamond Sutra, repented, and wished that the beings he had killed might soon be born in the Pure Land. The stated wish concerns those beings; it should not be replaced with an unrecorded prediction about his own rebirth.

At eighty-one, half a month before his death, he invited relatives and friends to share a vegetarian meal on the ninth day of the eleventh lunar month. The account says that after the meal he bathed, changed clothes, sat upright, composed a verse, and died.

The source does not explain how he earned a living after leaving slaughter. Nor does its narrator explicitly declare that he had been reborn in Sukhāvatī. This draft preserves those limits while following the connection between a change of occupation, learning anew, and sustained practice.

## Editorial reflection (not source text)

A starting point may be to identify a form of harm one wishes to reduce and consider a responsible, sustainable change. Livelihood and family responsibilities require attention to present circumstances; the text does not supply a ready-made career plan.
